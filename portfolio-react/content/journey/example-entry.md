This entry exists to show the shape of the format and to prove the pipeline —
the product card below, the related reading, the RSS item and the structured
data are all real. **The content is illustrative. Delete this file and write
your own.**

## Context

Open with the situation. What were you working on, and what did the thing look
like before you touched it? A reader who has never seen the product needs this
to follow the rest.

## The problem

What was actually wrong. Be specific — "the conversion step was confusing" is
weaker than describing the moment a user got stuck and what they did next.

## What I was trying to achieve

State the goal plainly, and the constraint you were working under. Constraints
are usually the more interesting half.

## The hypothesis

What you believed would happen, written before you knew the answer. Recording
the prediction is what separates an experiment from a story told backwards.

## What I changed

The actual change, at whatever level of detail is useful:

- What was added, removed or reworked
- Why that approach over the alternatives
- What you deliberately did not do

## What happened

The outcome, including the parts that went badly. An entry that reports only
successes is marketing, and readers can tell.

## What I learned

The transferable part — the thing you would tell someone starting the same
work. This is usually the reason anyone shares the piece.

## What I would do differently

Hindsight, honestly. This section builds more credibility than any claim about
results.

## What's next

The follow-up experiment, or the open question you have not resolved. It gives
the next entry somewhere to start.
